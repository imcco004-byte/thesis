# The master put the slave to work on/under the non-essential negative object. the experience the slave gains from working on/under the object allows them to seperate themselves from the object and achieves fulfillment of the original desire of the master as a living being( hegelian life and desire, hegel defines them strictly). the master, the slave, and the non-essential negative object are all different.

Self-consciousness attains its satisfaction only in another self-consciousness the master, the servant, and the thing are each a self-sufficient moment, but the truth of self-sufficiency proves to be servile consciousness. [^1_1]

Self-consciousness, as desire, first posits the object as negative and tries to consume it, but this satisfaction is a vanishing without stability. [^1_1] The object which withstands desire as life is a living self-sufficiency, and thus self-consciousness attains its satisfaction only in another self-consciousness. [^1_1]

In the struggle for recognition, each risks his life in order to prove that freedom is the essence and that nothing in its immediate existence counts as an absolute. [^1_1] The one who does not carry the negation of immediate existence through to the end, who cannot ignore the chain of life, proves himself non-self-sufficient and becomes the servant, while the other becomes the master. [^1_1]

The master is the self-consciousness existing-for-itself which relates itself to the thing and to the other through a mediating middle the servant. [^1_1] He relates to the servant through self-sufficient being, which is the servants chain, and to the thing through the servants negating work, so that he enjoys the pure negation of the thing as consumption without labor. [^1_1]

However, the truth of the self-sufficient consciousness is the servile consciousness, since the masters object is not a self-sufficient self-consciousness but a non-self-sufficient one, and his certainty therefore does not correspond to the concept of being-for-itself. [^1_1] The servant, in fear of death, experiences the dissolution of every fixed determinateness, the pure movement in which all stable existence becomes absolutely fluid this fear is the pure being-for-itself of self-consciousness in itself. [^1_1]

In service, the servant works off his natural existence, but this negation at first is only in itself. [^1_1] In formative activity or work, desire is held in check, the negative relation to the thing becomes a form which endures, and the servants own being-for-itself becomes objective for him as a self-sufficient shape. [^1_1]

In forming the thing, he destroys the external form of the alien essence before which he trembled and posits himself as this negative within a lasting existence he thus becomes for himself an existing being-for-itself. [^1_1] For this reflection both fear and service, as well as formative activity, are necessary fear without service remains inward and mute, and work without the absolute fear of death yields only stubbornness and a mind of ones own which is mere vanity. [^1_1]

Thus the servant, in fear and work on the inessential negative thing, separates himself from thinghood and comes to know himself as the essence, while the masters enjoyment of the thing and of the recognition of a non-self-sufficient consciousness fails to realize the concept of self-sufficient being-for-itself. [^1_1]

To make this even sharper in Marx’s critical spirit, one can compress the movement as follows using only Hegelian terms the master, whose truth should be pure being-for-itself, in fact makes the thing and the other do his own negating, and so his being-for-itself is mediated by an inessential consciousness and by thinghood. [^1_1] The servant, who appears as mere being-for-another and bound to the thing, through fear and culturally formative activity discovers in the negative work on the thing his own pure negativity as his essence and thus becomes the realized self-sufficiency which the master only claims to be. [^1_1]
